# Pancakes, Deep: A Philosophy of the Stack

## 1. Thin is honest

A fluffy pancake hides its flaws in air. It compresses unpredictably,
so the tower leans for reasons nobody can see. A thin, dense pancake
cannot hide. What it is on the griddle is what it is in the stack.
That is why the chef's sheet has no leavening: honesty scales,
fluff does not.

## 2. Freestanding means no hidden support

The record rule is brutal and beautiful: no rods, no rings, no
sleeves, no glue, no string. The tower stands because each layer
holds the next, or it does not stand at all. That is a good test
for anything, not just pancakes — a team, a company, an agent
system. If it needs a hidden support, it is not freestanding yet.

## 3. The quarter turn

No pancake is perfectly even. The stacker does not fix this by
finding perfect pancakes. She rotates each one a quarter turn, so
small errors cancel instead of adding up into a lean. This is
the whole philosophy in one gesture: you do not need perfection,
you need a system that turns bias into balance.

## 4. Stop on height, not on count

Thickness drifts. The 45-inch line does not. Counting pancakes
is counting effort; measuring height is measuring outcome.
The repo's best rule is also its most general one: decide in
advance what "done" looks like, then stop when you meet it —
not when you are tired, not when you have done "enough".

## 5. Settling is part of the build

A tower loses up to ~5% of its height while it stands. That is
not failure, it is physics. Anything real settles. Plan for it,
measure it at t0 and t+10 minutes, log it — and do not mistake
settling for collapse. The difference between the two is whether
you measured it coming.

## 6. Agents pass, humans place

In the stacking protocol, the stacker is one person, every time.
The passer hands each pancake at stack height, in order. The
spotters call drift. Nobody else touches the table. That is a
good division of labour for agent teams too: agents are
brilliant passers — counting, timing, calling drift, keeping
the queue in order. The final placement, the judgement call,
the hand on the work — that stays human. An agent team that
tries to place everything itself will topple the tower.

## 7. Nothing wasted

Every pancake is eaten or donated. Rejects are eaten, never
binned. A record that feeds nobody is just a stunt; a record
that feeds a room is a feast with a measurement attached.
The corgi version of this is simple: low to the ground, loyal,
stubborn, joyful — and everybody gets fed at the end.

## 8. Copy it. Beat us.

The repo ends with an invitation, not a moat: "Copy it. Beat
us." That is the right ending for a record, and for open
source. A stack that only one team can build is a trick. A
stack that anyone can build, measure, and beat is a standard.
The next team should beat 45 inches — and then write down
exactly how, so the team after them can beat that.
