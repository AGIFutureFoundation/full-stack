# Three More Records for Corgi + Team Agents

These are **proposed attempts, not claimed records**. Each one reuses the
same thin, dense pancake from the chef's sheet (v2.0.0) and the same
no-support rule: nothing touches the stack but pancakes, nothing between
layers, everything eaten or donated afterwards.

Benchmarks below are publicly reported Guinness records, cited so the
next attempts can be measured honestly. The adjudicator has the final
word on any new attempt, as in RULES.md.

## Record 1 — Fastest freestanding 45-inch tower

- **The idea:** Same tower, same rules, timed. Agents run the clock,
  the count, and the plumb calls; humans do the stacking.
- **Current pace in this repo:** ~7 seconds per pancake, ~30 minutes
  for ~255 pancakes (STACKING.md).
- **Target to beat:** Build a freestanding 45 in tower, hold 5 seconds,
  in **under 25 minutes** — about 5.9 seconds per pancake, with no
  change to the recipe, no rings, no rods, no syrup.
- **Agent jobs:** Passer cadence calls every 10 pancakes, laser drift
  readouts every 10, a live height count, and an automatic stop call
  at 45 in. Agents never touch the tower.
- **What would prove it:** Start-to-stop video, height gauge in frame,
  5-second hold on camera, and the stack-of-10 thickness logged in
  TESTLOG.md afterwards.

## Record 2 — Most pancakes tossed and caught in 60 seconds, team

- **The idea:** Coordination, not muscle. A team tosses pancakes,
  agents count catches in real time from video.
- **Benchmark:** 140 tosses in one minute by one person (Brad Jolly,
  Sydney, 21 Feb 2012, as publicly reported).
- **Target to beat:** **200 clean tosses-and-catches in 60 seconds**
  by a team, each toss caught in its own pan, no pancake dropped
  twice, no pancake reused in the count.
- **Agent jobs:** One agent per tosser for counting, one referee
  agent for the master count, a shared 60-second clock, and a
  slow-motion replay check for disputed catches.
- **What would prove it:** One continuous 60-second video, every
  tosser in frame, every catch visible, count overlaid live.

## Record 3 — Longest line of pancakes

- **The idea:** Scale, laid flat. Pancakes edge to edge in a single
  unbroken line, measured end to end, then eaten or donated.
- **Benchmark:** 130.84 m (429 ft 3 in), Kenwood Ltd, Tottenham
  Hotspur Stadium, London, 25 Feb 2020, as publicly reported.
- **The math:** At 9 in (0.2286 m) per pancake, 130.84 m needs
  573 pancakes to pass it (130.84 ÷ 0.2286 = 572.3). At 7 in
  (0.1778 m) it needs 736.
- **Target to beat:** **A line over 131 m using the 9 in pancakes
  from this recipe — 573 or more, no gaps, no overlaps, no
  stacking, no supports.**
- **Agent jobs:** Layout planning (how many batches: 573 ÷ 3 per
  batch = 191 batches at 9 in), a measured tape line, drone or
  overhead photo for the full line, and a donation plan for
  every pancake in the line.
- **What would prove it:** Surveyor-style measurement photos at
  both ends, the full line in one overhead shot, and a count
  sheet tied to batch numbers.

## Why these three

- Speed (Record 1) tests the stacker under time pressure.
- Coordination (Record 2) tests the team, not one hand.
- Scale (Record 3) tests logistics, not height.
- Together they cover time, teamwork, and volume — the three
  things an agent team is actually good at: counting, timing,
  and keeping 500+ pancakes in order without losing count.

## Rules that carry over to all three

- Standard ingredients only, as the adjudicator asked (Oct 22).
- No hidden supports, no glue, no stringing.
- Every pancake edible and eaten or donated.
- Measured numbers go in TESTLOG.md. Guesses do not count.
